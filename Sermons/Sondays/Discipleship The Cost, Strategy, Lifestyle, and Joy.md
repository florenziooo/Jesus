## 1. Discipleship Is a Costly Command

Discipleship is not something we selectively practice according to convenience.

- We often love the good parts of the gospel:
    
    - forgiveness
        
    - reconciliation
        
    - blessings
        
    - miracles
        
- But discipleship becomes costly when Jesus says, **“Come, follow me.”**
    
- We can make excuses:
    
    - “Let me arrange this first.”
        
    - “I’m busy with my business.”
        
    - “I’m broken right now. I’m going to fix myself first.”
        
    - “I’m still waiting for a word or confirmation.”
        
- But Jesus gave the command to **go and make disciples**.
    

> “You have to be a faithful disciple. You know what faithful disciples do? They show up.”

### Discipleship Is a Two-Way Street

Discipleship applies both to disciple-makers and to disciples.

- If you are not yet ready to disciple others, first become a faithful disciple.
    
- Go through the necessary training and formation.
    
- A faithful disciple **shows up**.
    
- You cannot follow Jesus while continually being too busy with everything else.
    

> “Build it with Him. Let Him build it through you.”

Discipleship is no joke. It may look fun and engaging from the outside, but it is a **costly command**.

---

## 2. Discipleship Is God's Strategy for Kingdom Expansion

Church expansion and kingdom expansion are not necessarily the same thing.

- A church can expand through many churches or satellite churches while producing attenders rather than disciples.
    
- The goal is not merely to gather people but to produce disciples.
    

> “Discipleship is the best strategy for impartation.”

### Discipleship Produces Impartation

Discipleship is not only about imparting information from the Bible.

It also involves imparting:

- what you have perceived from the Lord
    
- the giftings you have
    
- the revelation you have received
    

This happens through the arrangement of discipleship.

### Jesus Modeled Multiplication

Jesus did not disciple the whole world individually.

- He selected and discipled a few.
    
- Those disciples became world changers.
    
- One person's transformation can lead to another person's transformation, then a family, friends, and eventually whole communities.
    

> “One person's change causes another person's change, and then to another person, and then to the whole family, and then to his friends.”

The goal is multiplication without needing a bloody revolution.

### Disciples Must Eventually Disciple Others

Discipleship should not remain permanently within a single disciple-leader relationship.

- The person being discipled must eventually be able to disciple another.
    
- What they received must be passed on and imparted to others.
    
- It is a **legacy**.
    

**2 Timothy 2:2 (NIV)**

> “And the things you have heard me say in the presence of many witnesses entrust to reliable people who will also be qualified to teach others.”

The preacher describes this as four-generation discipleship:

**Paul → Timothy → faithful people → others**

> “Discipled people disciple people.”

The full impact of our investment may not be visible immediately.

Like a seed:

- it is buried
    
- nurtured
    
- cultivated
    
- and eventually produces fruit

## 3. Discipleship Is a Lifestyle

Discipleship is not:

- an activity in church
    
- an event
    
- a series
    
- a campaign
    

> “Discipleship is a lifestyle.”

It should become part of who we are.

> “It should be woven into our spiritual veins.”

We cannot separate following Jesus from discipleship.

### Discipleship Requires Proximity

Some people prefer to remain at a distance:

- attend church
    
- listen to the pastor
    
- serve
    
- then go home
    

But discipleship involves proximity and relationship.

People may avoid proximity because they do not want their mess to be seen.

They may feel:

- too messy
    
- too chaotic
    
- too broken
    

Or they may simply be unwilling to be held. Pride can also be involved.

But we are **not meant to walk this life alone**.

God has given us:

- family
    
- laborers
    
- encouragers
    
- leaders
    
- disciplers
    

because every day brings attacks and opposition.

### The Enemy Wants to Return Us to Old Patterns

Once we give our lives to Jesus, we should expect opposition.

The enemy does not want us to obey. He wants us to return to our old patterns so that we do not live free.

Therefore, discipleship must become an everyday lifestyle.

It does not mean abandoning your job or responsibilities.

It means:

- managing your time
    
- saying no to opportunities that prevent kingdom participation
    
- recognizing that not every opportunity with good income is necessarily an opportunity that advances kingdom purposes
    

> “Your income, that big, but disables you to participate in the kingdom expansion to disciples you. It's not gonna get you somewhere. You need this.”

### Do Not Be Overwhelmed—Be Challenged

The preacher distinguishes between being overwhelmed and being challenged.

> “Don’t get overwhelmed. I would rather have you feel challenged. Not overwhelmed. Be challenged.”

Discipleship pushes us beyond our comfort zone.

- We need leaders and disciplers to push us.
    
- We need to grow out of our comfort zone.
    
- We need to step into obedience.
    
- We should not become satisfied with our current reality.
    

> “Stop suckling with your current reality and accepting if this is already my life.”

Discipleship challenges entitlement and forces growth.

---

## 4. You Must Be Discipled Before You Can Make Disciples

Wanting to become a disciple-maker is good, but disciple-makers must first be faithful disciples.

> “You need to be discipled first before you can make one.”

### You Cannot Replicate What You Have Not Lived

A leader cannot effectively teach alignment, calibration, devotion, excellence, or obedience while refusing to live those things personally.

> “Only faithful disciples can produce true disciples.”

> “It takes one to make one.”

> “You cannot replicate a version of yourself you never lived out.”

Discipleship requires **alignment and integrity**.

For example:

- It is difficult to preach devotion when you do not practice devotion.
    
- It is difficult to preach excellence while continually making excuses.
    
- It is difficult to preach alignment while refusing to align yourself with your leader.
    

The goal is not performance.

The preacher emphasizes that excellence should become an overflow of our relationship with Jesus because the Spirit of God makes us excellent.

### Compromise Becomes a Problem

> “A place of compromise is the devil’s playground.”

Compromise creates tension and conflict.

When someone is living in compromise, correction can begin to feel like an attack rather than an opportunity for discipleship.

But Jesus did not only command us to make disciples.

He also commanded us to teach them to obey.

Therefore, **teaching is a crucial part of discipleship.**

## 5. The Cost of Following Jesus

**Matthew 16:24–25 (NIV)**

> “Whoever wants to be my disciple must deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me. For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me will find it.”

The cost of being a disciple of Jesus is to **deny yourself every day**.

It means:

- surrendering your life
    
- surrendering your wants
    
- surrendering your dreams
    
- putting God first
    
- choosing obedience over self
    

> “Your life, your wants, your dreams are surrendered to Him.”

### Take Up Your Cross

The cross represents death.

To take up your cross means to die to yourself.

This becomes especially difficult when:

- you are offended
    
- people hurt you
    
- you feel you are right
    
- you feel entitled to your position
    

Even when you believe you are on the right side of an argument, you still have to deny yourself.

> “Most especially when you're offended. You gotta deny yourself.”

### Lordship Means Putting Jesus First

Following Jesus means lordship.

The preacher emphasizes that Jesus must come first, even in relation to family and personal priorities.

**Luke 14:26 (NIV)**

> “If anyone comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters—yes, even their own life—such a person cannot be my disciple.”

The point presented in the sermon is that Jesus must have first place.

This does not mean compromising or abandoning family.

Rather, we should seek to win and bless our families so that they understand what lordship looks like.

### The Cost Includes Suffering

Following Jesus can involve:

- persecution
    
- rejection
    
- sacrificial love
    
- giving your time
    
- giving your money
    
- giving your presence
    
- serving people
    
- obedience
    
- suffering
    

> “If you want to follow Jesus, you're going to have to suffer with Him.”

Grace is freely given, and salvation is by grace through faith.

But **discipleship is costly**.

Jesus gave Himself on the cross, and His sacrifice should not leave us as the same people we were before.

> “What big decisions are you making for God?”

The challenge is not merely to give God:

- extra time
    
- spare money
    
- leftover attention
    

The sacrifice of Jesus calls us to make significant decisions for Him.

## 6. Discipleship Is Stewardship, Not Ownership

Jesus' command to make disciples must be understood together with the responsibility to care for them.

### Matthew 28:19–20 — The Great Commission

**Matthew 28:19–20 (NIV)**

> “Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptising them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you.”

The preacher connects this with **John 21**.

### John 21 — Feed My Sheep

Jesus repeatedly asks Peter whether he loves Him, and each time gives him a responsibility:

- “Feed my lambs.”
    
- “Take care of my sheep.”
    
- “Feed my sheep.”
    

The preacher presents John 21 as a counterpart to Matthew 28.

|Matthew 28|John 21|
|---|---|
|Make disciples|Take care of disciples|
|Reach people|Shepherd people|
|Evangelism|Nurturing|
|Multiply|Mature|
|Empowering mission|Stewardship|

The point is that we cannot continually win people while failing to love and nurture the people we have already discipled.

> “Discipleship is stewardship. Not ownership.”

### Jesus' Sheep Belong to Jesus

Jesus said:

> “Feed my sheep.”

Not:

- your sheep
    
- your lambs
    

They belong to Jesus.

Therefore, leaders are accountable to God for how they care for the people entrusted to them.

We must not treat disciples as statistics or possessions.

### Do Not Control Your Disciples

Discipleship does not mean making decisions for people.

- Give them perspective.
    
- Explain the implications of their decisions.
    
- Help them seek God.
    
- But allow them room to decide.
    

The preacher emphasizes that even God does not overtake man's free will.

True repentance must come from the person's own decision before God, not because a disciple leader forced them.

Likewise, disciples should not become completely dependent on their leader's voice.

> “You have access to the Word of God.”

> “You have access to the voice of God.”

Leaders must not condition people to become dependent upon them.

> “If you're a leader and you're conditioning your people to be dependent on you, stop doing that. You are manipulating them.”

Healthy discipleship gives people room to:

- decide
    
- seek the Lord
    
- hear from God
    
- grow in maturity
    

### Invest Without Demanding a Return

We must intentionally invest in people's lives even when:

- there is no immediate return
    
- they cannot reciprocate
    
- there is no visible progress yet
    

We are sowing seeds.

A seed does not grow immediately.

It may take:

- a month
    
- a year
    
- two years
    
- three years
    

**Galatians 6:9 (NIV)**

> “Let us not become weary in doing good, for at the proper time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up.”

## 7. The Practical Lifestyle of a Disciple

The preacher describes several areas in which discipleship becomes an everyday lifestyle:

### 1. People Life

Doing life with people and sharing life together.

### 2. Survival Life

Learning to navigate everyday realities while remaining faithful to Jesus.

### 3. Church Life

Faithfully participating in church.

### 4. Training Life

Participating in formation such as:

- Life Class
    
- School of Destiny
    
- other discipleship training
    

### 5. Evangelism Life

Sharing the gospel with:

- strangers
    
- friends
    
- people around us
    

Evangelism does not always require having a perfect script.

Sometimes blessing a friend or stranger can open their heart.

> “Sometimes blessing your friends is how you share the gospel.”

> “Sometimes that's the only way to get to their heart.”

### Day-to-Day Discipleship

**Acts 5:42 (NIV)**

> “Day after day, in the temple courts and from house to house, they never stopped teaching and proclaiming the good news that Jesus is the Messiah.”

The preacher emphasizes a lifestyle of continual discipleship, church, training, devotion, and evangelism.

## 8. The Goal of Discipleship Is Christlikeness

The ultimate goal is not merely:

- having more people in church
    
- having excellent servants
    
- having gifted people
    
- producing impressive ministry
    

The goal is **Christlikeness**.

> “The goal of our discipleship is Christ's likeness.”

We disciple people because we want them to reflect the character of Christ.

Jesus demonstrated radical love—even toward people who persecuted and hated Him.

He did not only model how to live like Him.

He also modeled how to **die like Him**.

True love involves laying down one's life for another.

> “True love is when you're able to leave and forsake everything behind for another person's interest. For another person's salvation.”

### We Share Not Only the Gospel but Our Lives

**1 Thessalonians 2:8 (NIV)**

> “Because we loved you so much, we were delighted to share with you not only the gospel of God but our lives as well.”

Discipleship therefore involves **doing life together**.

It does not always require a formal event.

Sometimes a simple invitation and an important conversation can be discipleship.

## 9. The Joy of Discipleship

The cost of discipleship is real, but there is also joy on the other side of the cost.

### Joy #1 — Transformation

The joy of a leader is seeing the people they disciple transformed.

- renewed
    
- changed
    
- no longer walking the same path as before
    
- increasingly Christlike
    

The leader's joy is no longer centered on:

- success
    
- money
    
- personal achievement
    

The joy becomes seeing people transformed.

### Joy #2 — Multiplication

We cannot change the world by ourselves.

We change the world **one life at a time**.

One transformed person can affect:

- a family
    
- a campus
    
- a workplace
    
- a community
    
- a nation
    

> “Multiplication is our joy.”

Healthy things grow.

Multiplication demonstrates that discipleship is producing healthy disciples who can disciple others.

> “Disciples, families, campuses, workplaces, nations.”

The vision presented is multiplication beyond the existing church community.

The preacher calls the church to believe for growth while refusing to become comfortable.

> “We can't keep comfortable. We have to pay the price. We have to pay the cost.”

### Joy #3 — Seeing People Walk in Truth

**3 John 4 (NIV)**

> “I have no greater joy than to hear that my children are walking in the truth.”

The preacher connects this joy with seeing people transformed in their understanding and way of life.

The Word of God remains the ultimate authority of truth.

Even when culture changes, the Word of God remains the same.

The preacher emphasizes loving people while holding to biblical truth.

### Joy #4 — The People We Disciple

**1 Thessalonians 2:19–20 (NIV)**

> “For what is our hope or joy or the crown in which we will glory in the presence of our Lord Jesus when he comes? Is it not you? Indeed, you are our glory and joy.”

The disciples themselves become the joy and glory of those who invested in them.

> “Our joy is you.”

The stories and testimonies of transformed people remind leaders why they continue discipling.

Even when discipleship is:

- exhausting
    
- offensive
    
- difficult
    
- costly
    

it is worth it.

> “It’s worth it. It’s exhausting. It’s offensive, but it’s worth it.”

---

# Core Message

Discipleship is:

1. **A costly command** — following Jesus requires surrender, obedience, and sacrifice.
    
2. **God's strategy for kingdom expansion** — disciples make disciples, creating multiplication and legacy.
    
3. **A lifestyle** — discipleship is not an event or activity but part of everyday life.
    
4. **A process of formation** — faithful disciples must first be discipled before they can disciple others.
    
5. **A call to Christlikeness** — the goal is to reflect the character of Jesus.
    
6. **Stewardship, not ownership** — the people we disciple belong to Jesus, not to us.
    
7. **A call to sacrificial investment** — we sow into people even when the harvest is not immediately visible.
    
8. **A source of joy** — transformation, multiplication, and seeing people walk in truth make the cost worthwhile.
    

> “Transformation is worth your patience.”

> “Every disciple that is here... is worth fighting for. Worth contending for.”
