# Fatherlessness, the Orphan Spirit, and the Spirit of Adoption

## The Impact of Fatherlessness

There is a significant impact of **fatherlessness** in our country.

A fatherless society can **feminize the understanding of what a man should be**, because without healthy models of fatherhood, people can lose a proper understanding of identity, responsibility, authority, and what it means to be a man.

**Jesus came to earth to reveal the Father.**

> “Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father.” — John 14:9

Being saved does not automatically mean that every area of your life is already healed.

**Just because you’re saved doesn’t mean you’re healed.**

Salvation gives us a new identity in Christ, but there can still be wounds, mindsets, and patterns that need to be brought into the light and healed.

---

# The Orphan Spirit

An **orphan spirit** can manifest through a deep sense of:

- Loneliness
    
- Abandonment
    
- Isolation
    
- Rejection
    
- Unworthiness
    

It is a **broken person praying for love**.

If we are not careful, we can treat the church like an **orphanage**—constantly waiting to be picked, noticed, affirmed, or validated.

Instead of living from the identity of being a son or daughter, we begin **doing more in ministry to become something**.

> **Orphan mindset:** “If I do enough, maybe I will become worthy.”
> 
> **Sonship:** “Because I am already loved, I can serve from that identity.”

## Orphan Tendencies

The orphan mindset can manifest in different ways.

### 1. The Victim

A person may manifest as a **victim**.

They may try to numb their pain through vices or unhealthy coping mechanisms.

Instead of dealing with the wound, they use something else to temporarily escape it.

---

### 2. The Persecutor

A person may manifest as a **persecutor**.

This can lead to abusiveness, becoming a troublemaker, or constantly being defensive.

They always feel the need to **protect themselves**.

Their aggression can become a defense mechanism—a way of coping with the pain and fear underneath.

---

### 3. The Rescuer

A person may manifest as a **rescuer**.

They constantly try to prove that they are worthy by helping others.

They become **slaves to performance**.

They genuinely want to help people, but they can have difficulty recognizing their **own need for help**.

Their identity becomes:

> “I am valuable because I am useful.”

---

# The Prodigal Brother and the Orphan Mindset

The story of the prodigal son also reveals the orphan mindset through the **older brother**.

When his younger brother returned and was celebrated by the father, the older brother became **angry**.

His response revealed something deeper: although he was already in the father's house, he was not living with the heart of a son.

### Anger

**“Be angry and do not sin.”**

Anger itself can become an entry point to sin when we allow it to govern our hearts.

Unresolved anger can open us to destructive spiritual patterns and cause us to authorize sin over our hearts.

### Isolation

When we are hurt, we may begin to believe:

> “Nobody understands me.”

We become convinced by the enemy's narrative that **we are not worthy to be listened to**.

So we withdraw.

We walk away and choose to be alone.

But sometimes, isolation is not simply something that happened to us—we begin **making laws for ourselves**:

- “Nobody will understand me.”
    
- “I don't need anyone.”
    
- “I will handle this myself.”
    
- “People will only hurt me anyway.”
    

What began as pain becomes a self-imposed way of living.

### From Isolation to Entitlement

The older brother eventually manifested **entitlement**.

He believed that because of everything he had done, he deserved something from the Father.

This reveals another aspect of the orphan mindset:

> **“After everything I have done, I deserve to be treated differently.”**

Instead of relating to the Father from sonship, he related to Him through performance and entitlement.

### Jealousy

The older brother became jealous when his brother was celebrated.

Instead of rejoicing that his brother had returned, he compared himself to him.

This can happen when our identity is based on **what we receive compared to others**.

When someone else is celebrated, an orphan mindset asks:

> “What about me?”

But sonship allows us to celebrate others without feeling that their blessing diminishes our own place in the Father's house.

---

# Jesus Does Not Leave Us as Orphans

Jesus said:

> **“I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you.”**  
> — John 14:18

Jesus promised the coming of the **Holy Spirit**.

The Holy Spirit resides in us, reminding us that we are not abandoned and that we belong to God.

We are not spiritual orphans waiting to be picked.

**We have a Father.**

---

# The Spirit of Adoption

The answer to the orphan mindset is **sonship and adoption**.

When we are adopted into God's family:

### 1. We Belong

We are no longer outsiders.

We have a place in the Father's house.

### 2. We Cannot Be Disowned

When we are adopted as God's children, our identity is not based on our performance.

We do not have to constantly prove that we deserve to remain in the family.

### 3. We Have an Inheritance

An adopted child has a **share in the inheritance**.

We don't have to fight for scraps of acceptance.

We are children of the Father, and therefore we have an inheritance in Him.

---

# From Orphanhood to Sonship

The orphan mindset says:

> **“I need to perform so that I can become worthy.”**

Sonship says:

> **“I am already loved, therefore I can serve.”**

The orphan asks:

> “Will I be accepted?”

The son knows:

> “I belong.”

The orphan competes.

The son celebrates.

The orphan isolates.

The son belongs to a family.

The orphan performs for love.

The son serves **from love**.

The orphan fights for an inheritance.

The son knows **he already has an inheritance**.

**Jesus came to reveal the Father and to bring us from orphanhood into sonship.**
