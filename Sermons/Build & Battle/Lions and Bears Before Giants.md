([[1 Samuel 16]]–[[1 Samuel 17]])

### 1. The Giant, and a King Anointed in Secret

Goliath had defied the armies of Israel for forty days — a seasoned warrior, described (through Saul's own words) as "a warrior from his youth." Israel, though a nation that had won many battles before, froze. No one stepped forward.
Meanwhile, David had already been anointed privately as the next king ([[1 Samuel 16]]), but the anointing did not send him to the palace. It sent him back to the pasture.

> [!quote] [[1 Samuel 17#^v15|1 Samuel 17:15]] NIV
> "but he went back and forth from Saul to tend his father's sheep at Bethlehem."

- David didn't rush the process or skip a step because he'd been anointed.
- The first time David actually entered the palace, it wasn't to rule — it was to serve Saul, who was tormented in spirit.

### 2. The Source of David's Confidence

When David finally speaks to Saul about facing Goliath, he doesn't appeal to skill or strength. He appeals to history with God.

> [!quote] [[1 Samuel 17#^v34|1 Samuel 17:34–36]] NIV
> "But David said to Saul, 'Your servant has been keeping his father's sheep. When a lion or a bear came and carried off a sheep from the flock, I went after it, struck it and rescued the sheep from its mouth. When it turned on me, I seized it by its hair, struck it and killed it. Your servant has killed both the lion and the bear; this uncircumcised Philistine will be like one of them, because he has defied the armies of the living God.'"

- David is not saying "I'm the best warrior you'll ever have." He is revealing where his confidence, his peace, his anchor actually came from.
- It wasn't based on weapons, armor, or ability — it was based on what he had personally seen God do before, even when no one else witnessed it.
3. Don't Trade Faithfulness for a Goliath to Kill
The preacher warns against becoming so fixated on "killing your Goliath" — chasing the big breakthrough, the platform, the spotlight — that you lose faithfulness where you currently stand.
"David was not looking for his Goliath... he was just faithfully doing his assignment."
- David's assignment that season wasn't to be a warrior — it was to tend sheep.
- "Assignments are still assignments. Assignments still require faithfulness" — regardless of room, platform, or whether you have a microphone.
- Promotion is often delayed because pursuit of presence with God gets substituted for pursuit of the "Goliath" — the big moment we want to make happen for ourselves.
4. Preparation Happens Where No One Is Watching
David's development as a future king happened in obscurity, not on a battlefield anyone saw.
"God sees you. God sees the unseen places... before God gives you a public platform, He is watching your private faithfulness."
"The real venture for maturity is not public credibility, but consistency in private."
The preacher connects this to a broader warning: many chase recognition and validation because of unmet affirmation elsewhere, and settle for performance over substance — illustrated with the lamp-and-oil picture:
"We focus on the lamp and forgot the oil."
- People can preach, sing, or play an instrument well and still lack "oil" — the substance that actually matures and raises up others.
- The question isn't "can you perform?" but "is your torch still burning for Jesus?"
5. The Progression: Lion, Bear, Giant
The battles escalate in size, and each one deepens what David knows about God:
- The lion taught David: my God can deliver me.
- The bear taught David: my God will deliver me.
- The giant confirmed: my God has delivered me before — I can trust Him again.
"God develops people where nobody sees them... nobody Instagrams the crushing."
- If you're still facing the same lion, the same bear, with nothing changing, something needs to change.
- Past deliverances aren't meant to be trophies you display — they're the confidence you carry into the next battle.
6. Your Private Lions and Bears
The preacher applies the lion and bear directly to hidden, personal struggles — the battles no one sees or applauds: secret temptation, anger, addiction, pride, lust, comparison, bitterness.
"Some men are strong publicly, but they're losing privately."
"The question is not who are you in church? The question is... who are you when nobody's watching?"
7. What's Really Being Attacked Isn't You — It's What's Entrusted to You
The lion and bear didn't attack David directly; they attacked the flock — "my father's sheep."
"The enemy doesn't care about you. He's after what God has entrusted to you."
- This is applied to marriage, children, disciples, calling, and destiny — what God has placed in a person's care.
- Stewardship produces courage: David fought because the sheep weren't his — they belonged to his father. Understanding something is entrusted, not owned, is what makes it worth fighting for.
- The preacher connects this to identity: "The enemy will never bother you unless you're ignorant... the moment you discover who God made you to be, now you're a threat."
8. Battles Nobody Celebrates
A list of everyday, unseen faithfulness that the preacher says matters even without recognition:
- Choosing purity
- Getting up early to pray when no one applauds it
- Saying sorry
- Choosing integrity when no one would know otherwise
- Saying no to opportunities that would compromise your faith
- Raising children spiritually
- Serving when no one says thank you
"There are battles that you fight that nobody celebrates publicly... and you're going to be okay with that."
9. Goliath Wasn't Where Courage Was Developed — It's Where Courage Was Demonstrated
By the time David stood before Goliath, his courage was already formed.
"Goliath was not where David developed his courage. Goliath was when David demonstrated his courage."
- His confidence rested on his history with God, not on Saul's armor, which he tried on and rejected because it was untested.
- David went with what had already worked in the field — the sling and stone, the tools he'd been trained with by God.
"It's not the weapon, it's God."
- Applied to leaders: new levels and new seasons will not be won with old strategies — they require recognizing when God is opening a new door and shifting accordingly.
10. A Legacy of Giants — Teaching the Next Generation
The preacher offers a connection he explicitly frames as speculative — not stated directly in Scripture, but "a possibility": Bethlehem sits near Hebron, historically known as "the city of giants," where Caleb fought and drove out giants ([[Joshua 14]]).

> [!quote] [[Deuteronomy 3#^v11|Deuteronomy 3:11]] NIV
> "Og king of Bashan was the last of the Rephaites. His bed was decorated with iron and was more than nine cubits long and four cubits wide. It is still in Rabbah of the Ammonites."

- Point: giants were already part of the region's history — David wasn't the first to face one.

> [!quote] [[Deuteronomy 6#^v6|Deuteronomy 6:6–7]] NIV
> "These commandments that I give you today are to be on your hearts. Impress them on your children. Talk about them when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up."

> [!quote] [[Psalm 78#^v5|Psalm 78:5–7]] NIV
> "He decreed statutes for Jacob and established the law in Israel, which he commanded our ancestors to teach their children, so the next generation would know them, even the children yet to be born, and they in turn would tell their children. Then they would put their trust in God and would not forget his deeds but would keep his commands."

- Applied teaching: fathers carry a responsibility not just to provide, but to pass down the testimony of God's faithfulness to their children.
- Importantly, David's own confidence wasn't secondhand: he says "the God who delivered me," not "the God who delivered them." His faith was rooted in personal encounter, not inherited history alone.

> [!quote] [[1 Samuel 17#^v26|1 Samuel 17:26]] NIV
> "...he should defy the armies of the living God."

- David's question about "this uncircumcised Philistine" is explained by the preacher as pointing out that Goliath stood outside covenant with God, while Israel's armies stood in covenant with Yahweh — the real issue wasn't Goliath's size, but whose armies he was defying.
11. The Battle That Triggers Destiny — and the Legacy You Leave
The reward tied to defeating Goliath was significant:

> [!quote] [[1 Samuel 17#^v25|1 Samuel 17:25]] NIV
> "He will also give him his daughter in marriage."

- The preacher's point: the battle didn't create David's destiny — it triggered it. It opened a door already appointed for him.
- Some battles are like this: they don't manufacture your future, but they release you into it.
Looking ahead to the next generation:
"The greatest gift you can give your children are finished battles that belong to your time."
- David fought and finished the giants of his generation so that Solomon's generation could focus on building rather than repeating the same fights.
- Warning to single men specifically: don't move into the next season without defeating the battles that belong to the current one — what isn't finished gets passed on, whether as victory or as unfinished giants.

Closing Application
The message closes with an invitation to those currently in "lion and bear" seasons — private struggle, frustration, feeling close to quitting — to bring that to God rather than waiting for a public platform to be faithful. The appeal: build a history with God now, in the hidden place, because that history is what will carry someone through when the giant finally shows up.
