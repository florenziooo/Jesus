([[1 Samuel 18]]–[[1 Samuel 24]])
Recap: From Shepherd to Giant-Killer ([[1 Samuel 17]])
The previous session covered David's preparation before Goliath — killing the bear and the lion. In chapter 17, David is the only one courageous enough to face Goliath, kills him, and overnight becomes the biggest celebrity in the nation. Saul asks, "Whose son are you, young man?" and David answers, "I am the son of your servant, Jesse the Bethlehemite."
Chapter 18 picks up immediately — there is no time gap. It is a direct continuation of that same moment.

### 1. A Chosen Brother: David and Jonathan ([[1 Samuel 18#^v1|1 Samuel 18:1–4]])

As soon as David finished speaking to Saul, "the soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul." Saul kept David in his house and would not let him return home. Jonathan made a covenant with David because he loved him as his own soul.
- David enters this new season of success not alone, but with a chosen brother — not a blood relative, but a covenant relationship.
- No man can live as an island. Success, faithfulness, and strength require men around you.
- Jonathan will prove essential to keeping David alive through the chapters that follow.
- There will be seasons where it's just you and God — David fought the lion with no one at his back — but that is not meant to describe your whole life.
"That is true. But that is not the case for your life... It cannot be true for your life."
Application: If you are isolated or lonely, you need to get connected — to a cell group, to men who are speaking into your life, to people who are covering you.

2. The Danger of a Jealous Heart: Saul's Turn ([[1 Samuel 18#^v6|1 Samuel 18:6–9]])
As David returns from striking down the Philistine, the women come out singing and dancing: "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands." Saul is angry — "What more can he have but the kingdom?"
- The moment celebrity and applause begin, Saul's heart changes toward David.
- It doesn't matter what David has accomplished — when someone's heart is in a lost place, anything can turn them against you.
- The preacher pauses to reflect: in reading these stories, it's natural to focus on David and ask what we can learn from him. But some of us are David, and some of us are Saul.
"I never want to be Saul. I never want to allow myself to be intimidated and jealous of the men whom God is raising up after me."
- Insecurity is described as a "you" problem to address — not by handling it alone, but by bringing it to leaders and to the Lord.
"Nothing will kill your capacity faster than insecurity, then jealousy."

### 3. Serving the King Under Pressure ([[1 Samuel 18#^v10|1 Samuel 18:10–11]])

The next day, a harmful spirit from God rushed on Saul, and he raved in his house while David played the lyre for him, as he did day by day. Saul hurled his spear, thinking, "I will pin David to the wall" — but David evaded him, not once, but twice.
Consider where David is found: he is the war hero, the biggest celebrity in the nation, the subject of the women's song declaring him greater than the king — and yet he is found serving the king, playing music, bringing peace.
"The kingdom is built on humility and a servant's heart. Our masculinity isn't complete without humility and a servant's heart. We must be found serving the king."
- No matter your status — celebrity, CEO, top of your industry — you are called to be found humbling yourself and serving.
- Even when the king is trying to kill him, David is still found serving him.
"Get over yourself."
Application: Some quit because a boss doesn't understand them, or a teacher doesn't like them. No matter what, we should be found serving the king, humbling ourselves before him.

4. An Anointing That Bothers the Wrong People ([[1 Samuel 18#^v12|1 Samuel 18:12–16]])
"Saul was afraid of David because the Lord was with him, but had departed from Saul."
- Saul recognizes what he sees in David because he once carried it himself — he remembers his own anointing by Samuel.
- Because Saul can't handle it, he removes David from his presence and makes him commander of a thousand. David has success in all his undertakings, "for the Lord was with him."
"Humility and servanthood, and the anointing of God will bother people in your life whose hearts are not with you, and whose hearts are not with God."
- If people can't be around you because you're annoying, insensitive, or a jerk — that's a character issue; fix it.
- But if you are walking in the call of God, there will be people who simply can't stand you, and it isn't yours to fix.
"Not everybody is worth pursuing in the same way... not everyone is supposed to be in our circle." Some are to be loved from afar, with grace and mercy.
By verse 14–15, David has success in all his undertakings, and Saul "stood in fearful awe of him" — but all Israel and Judah loved David.

### 5. Four Chapters on the Run

What follows (paraphrased across several chapters) is described as a back-and-forth: Saul, unable to manage his emotions, repeatedly tries to kill David. Jonathan has to warn David more than once, "My dad's going to kill you, brother." Saul eventually pursues David through the countryside with an army of thousands, until a messenger warns that the Philistines have invaded — forcing Saul to break off pursuit. That location becomes known as the Rock of Escape.

6. Into the Wilderness: En Gedi and the Cave ([[1 Samuel 23#^v29|1 Samuel 23:29–24]]:5)
David goes up and lives in the strongholds of En Gedi — a wilderness place, mostly rock and cave, largely barren, the kind of place no one would choose to live, yet full of hiding places.
Saul, having returned from the Philistines, takes 3,000 chosen men to seek David near the wild goats' rocks. He comes to the sheepfolds, where there is a cave, and goes in — while David and his men are hiding in the innermost parts of that same cave. David's men tell him:
"Here is the day of which the LORD said to you, Behold, I will give your enemy into your hand, and you shall do to him as it shall seem good to you."
David stealthily cuts off the corner of Saul's robe — and immediately afterward, his heart strikes him for having done it.
Reflection: There will be times you feel you've done everything right — served faithfully, been obedient, taken care of what was given to you — and still find yourself in a wilderness place, in the cave, wondering how you got there. The world can feel like it's against you even while you're doing the right thing.
"We can't equate comfort and calling."
For many, the more faithful the walk, the more the wilderness and the fire will show up — not less.
