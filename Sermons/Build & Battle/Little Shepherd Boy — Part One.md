Little Shepherd Boy — Part One
David: A Man After God’s Own Heart
- David had many mistakes, many ups and downs, but he is recalled as a man after God’s own heart.
- Not because he doesn’t fail, but because when he does, he goes back to the Lord.
- We look at David not to emulate the losses, but to emulate what he did when he lost.
- David is a warrior and a poet before the Lord. He is a worshiper.
- He can fight lions and bears, go to war and kill people, but at the same time, he is a warrior poet.
God Looks at the Heart
“For the Lord sees not as man sees. Man looks on the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart.”
- We all want to be impressed with people based on our personal standards.
- So often our lives, we anoint people for tasks in our influence and in our lives because of what we see on the outside.
- We look at the outside in relationships. We look at the outside for partners. We look at the outside in so many things in life.
- We need to judge how God judges.
- Even Samuel, the prophet of the Lord, gets caught up in what he sees on the outside.
- Samuel sees Eliab and thinks, “surely the Lord’s anointed is before him.”
- The Lord corrects him: “No, no, whoa, slow down.”
- It doesn’t matter who you are. It doesn’t matter if you have a direct line to God. You still can get caught up in what you see.
David Is Anointed, But Saul Is Still King
- Saul was the king, but he had stepped past his boundary as king and disobeyed the commands of God.
- God tells Samuel to anoint the next king and leads him to David’s house, the house of Jesse.
- David is anointed by the prophet of the Lord.
- His brothers and his father witnessed the prophet of God anoint him to be the next king.
“There is still a king on the throne. But the king is no longer anointed. David is anointed, but is yet not.”
- You would think that the next day, it’s David’s turn to step up and be king.
- But that is not how the story goes.
The Kingdom Does Not Operate With Usurpers
- David is anointed king, and his first step is not going to the palace.
- There is no celebration.
- There’s no red carpet.
- There’s nothing special.
“I don’t care how talented you are, how anointed you might be. Even if you are the next king, you are still commanded to serve the current one.”
“The kingdom does not operate with usurpers.”
“In the kingdom, we do not usurp the current kingdom and rush the process.”
- David is anointed as king, but his first step is to serve the current king.
- Whatever you have been anointed for, your first step, your only step, is to serve the king.
- Follow the process that God is leading you in.
- There is no skipping.
- There is no rushing.
- You have to serve the king before you step on the throne.
- David came to Saul and entered his service.
- He became Saul’s armor bearer.
- David worshiped and played to bring peace to the current king.
- It would have been easier for David to let Saul go crazy, be tormented, and then take the throne.
- But he humbles himself and becomes an armor bearer.
- He serves the current king even though he knows he is going to be the next king.
Do Not Accept a False Binary
- The Philistines show up.
- Goliath steps into the picture.
- Goliath gives Israel his terms of engagement: choose one man, let him fight, and let the result determine the outcome.
- But Israel had an army.
- They had two armies.
- They had the God of heaven and earth on their side.
- They had the current king and the chosen people of God.
“Why did Saul and the army of Israel accept the terms?”
- They accepted the terms of engagement from the enemy.
- They weren’t supposed to fight that way.
- Not one time in Israel’s history did one guy go up to a fight in this way.
- It was God sending His people and giving them favor for battle.
- It was never meant to be a showdown between one champion and the other.
“Do not accept the terms of engagement from the enemy.”
“Do not accept a false binary.”
- They were presented with a choice that was not the way they were supposed to fight.
- They accepted the terms and became afraid.
- Don’t accept the terms of engagement from the enemy. That’s where everything goes wrong.
David Speaks Faith in the Midst of Fear
- For 40 days, Goliath came forward and took his stand morning and evening.
- For 40 days, they accepted the terms of engagement and did nothing.
- All the men of Israel fled from him and were much afraid.
David asks:
“For who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?”
- David starts getting angry because Goliath is insulting God.
- David is not making it about himself.
- He is making it about God.
- He is not saying, “I can do this.”
- He is saying, “He is defying the armies of God.”
When You Speak Faith
- Eliab tells David to stay in his lane.
- David is questioned about why he isn’t with the sheep.
- The same brother who saw David being anointed as king is still telling him to stay in his lane.
“When you speak truth, people who are living in fear will be offended by you.”
“When you speak faith, those who live in fear will be offended by you.”
- They will tell you to stay in your lane.
- It is easy to tear down an idea with nothing to offer.
- Everybody is carrying David down and giving nothing in his place.
- “No, David, you can’t do it.”
- But if you can’t do it, then why don’t you do it?
David Had Been Tested in the Unseen
David tells Saul about the lion and the bear:
- He used to keep sheep for his father.
- When a lion or a bear came and took a lamb, he went after it.
- He struck it and delivered the lamb out of its mouth.
- He had already struck down both lions and bears.
“This uncircumcised Philistine shall be like one of them. For he has defied the armies of the living God.”
- David had already proved his skills when no one was watching.
- He had already been faithful in the unseen seasons.
“If you have proved yourself in the unseen seasons, don’t change just because it looks better in front of men.”
You Cannot Go Into Battle in Someone Else’s Armor
- Saul puts his armor on David.
- David tries to go, but he has not tested it.
“I cannot go with these, for I have not tested them.”
- You can’t go into battle in someone else’s armor.
- Armor is made in the image of the one who owns it.
- You cannot go into battle wearing the image of someone else.
- You cannot go into battle wearing the likeness of someone else.
- You cannot go into battle wearing the identity of someone else.
“You are called to fight how the Lord has called you to fight.”
- David already proved his skills when no one was watching.
- When you get before people and you’re in the big leagues, the temptation is to change it to impress people.
- “Now I gotta look like the king.”
- But David was meant to fight how he was meant to fight.
Do Not Take on the Identity of Those Who Doubt You
- Saul tells David he can’t do it.
- But if David is going to do it, Saul puts his armor on him.
- Saul does not believe in David, yet he covers David in his armor.
“Do not accept the identity of someone who God has rejected.”
“Be very slow to listen to the wisdom and the directive of people who don’t actually believe in you.”
“Be very slow” to put on the honor, identity, and image of those who don’t believe that you have what it takes.
- Do not bear the image of those who doubt you when you’re going to battle.
- David is not in this story for glory for himself.
- He has made it clear: it’s about God.
The Armor David Wore Was Faith
David says to Goliath:
“You come to me with sword and with a spear and with a javelin, but I come to you in the name of the Lord of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied.”
- Is that there for himself?
- The armor that David is wearing is faith.
- David goes into battle trusting in the Lord.
David runs quickly toward Goliath.
- He takes a stone.
- He slings it.
- The stone strikes Goliath on his forehead.
- Goliath falls on his face to the ground.
- David prevails over the Philistine with a sling and a stone.
