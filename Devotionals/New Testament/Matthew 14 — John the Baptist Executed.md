John was not afraid speaking of the truth.

5 “Herod wanted to kill John, but he was afraid of a riot, because all the people believed John was a prophet.”
In a couple Gospel accounts, Herod knew the truth that was revealed by John. Herod was intrigued—to the point that he decided not to kill John yet. Aside from that, he also considered that killing John would cause a riot.

However, after Herodias’ daughter’s compelling performance pleased Herod, he then lamented after learning that Samole wanted John’s head in a tray. But even though it was against Herod’s will, he killed John anyway for an oath.

This is a tragic story that tells: knowing the truth does not automatically mean obeying the truth.

For how long can we go living and preaching according to the truth? In times of pressure? When pride is about to shake?

Herod didn’t actually want to kill John. But because of his reckless oath and his people watching, he cared more about saving face rather than to face the truth.

The bible tells us that:
Speaking truth is always easier said than done, for living by the truth will cost you.
If we’re only doing it just because everyone is watching you, then what does that tell us about yourself?

Maybe I am performing to protect my reputation; not staying faithful even in our private and intimate moments with God.
It is very dangerous how pride can easily silence conviction.
