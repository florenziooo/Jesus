> [!quote] [[Matthew 14#^v24|Matthew 14:24]]
> **24** Meanwhile, the disciples were in trouble far away from land, for a strong wind had risen, and they were fighting heavy waves.

> [!quote] [[Matthew 14#^v25|Matthew 14:25]]
> **25** About three o’clock in the morning Jesus came toward them, walking on the water.

> [!quote] [[Matthew 14#^v26|Matthew 14:26]]
> **26** When the disciples saw him walking on the water, they were terrified. In their fear, they cried out, “It’s a ghost!”

> [!quote] [[Matthew 14#^v27|Matthew 14:27]]
> **27** But Jesus spoke to them at once. “Don’t be afraid,” he said. “Take courage. I am here!

In this moment, the disciples were into survival mode where they really had a long day and sleepless and were confronted by the storm and wind. And they just saw a human figure above water. Their brains aren’t thinking “That’s Jesus!” but they were thinking “That’s a ghost!”

But Jesus immediately comforted them by saying that He is here.

There are times were God approaches us in places we don’t recognize. And we often respond to Him in fear rather in rejoicing. We often forget that someone that we fear might be someone who had authority of things.

> [!quote] [[Matthew 14#^v28|Matthew 14:28-30]]
> **28** Then Peter called to him, “Lord, if it’s really you, tell me to come to you, walking on the water.”
> **29** “Yes, come,” Jesus said. So Peter went over the side of the boat and walked on the water toward Jesus.
> **30** But when he saw the strong wind and the waves, he was terrified and began to sink. “Save me, Lord!” he shouted.

Peter’s bold willingness to be tested in his faith shocked the rest of the disciples. He was already convinced that Jesus is really the Son of God. And that’s actually an attitude we must  installed in our hearts.

But as Peter continues to walk on the water, he then saw the strong wind and the waves, and began to sink.

This teaches a lesson: to have our eyes fixated on only Jesus.

The problem was Peter—though had the confidence of his faith—showed how little his faith was once he was distracted by the waves, or the wind, or the coldness of the water beneath his soles. He failed to have his eyes fixated on Jesus.

But even then especially when Peter failed, he surrendered, saying “Save me, Lord!” Jesus immediately reaches out his hand and saves him.

It’s not the waves, the winds, or the water that made Peter sink. It was his little faith.

And little faith is often found in places where we might expect great faith.
Little faith might be often revealed when we had the confidence to say but chicken out when things hit the fan.
That little faith that is far too eager for signs.
That little faith is very affected by what surrounds us.
And we often associate little faith as a weakness.

But Peter also revealed to us the strength of his little faith.
Because that same little faith is still true faith.
That same little faith that obeys the word of Jesus.
That same little faith struggles to come to Jesus.
That same little faith that prays when it is in trouble.
That same little faith is safe, because Jesus is near.

> [!quote] [[Matthew 14#^v31|Matthew 14:31]]
> **31** Jesus immediately reached out and grabbed him. “You have so little faith,” Jesus said. “Why did you doubt me?”

It is pretty reasonable question for Jesus to ask. 
Maybe some of us that why we doubt Jesus and His promises.
maybe you have found God unfaithful to His promise in the past.
maybe your friend told you that God can’t be trusted.
maybe your problem is too big for God to handle.
maybe you wonder if God has ever changed.

It is useful to confront our doubts.
Was there any good reason for your doubt?
Was there any good excuse for it?
Did any good come from your doubt.

If there be reason for little faith, there is evidently reason for great confidence. If it be right to trust Jesus at all, why not trust him altogether?
