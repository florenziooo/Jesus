**Central Scripture:**
> *'For no word from God will ever fail.'*  — Luke 1:37

When Zechariah fulfilled his once-in-a-lifetime duty to go into the temple of the Lord to burn incense, an angel appeared before him and that the Lord promised to give him a son called John—sent to make people prepared for the Lord.

> 'Zechariah asked the angel, “How can I be sure of this? I am an old man and my wife is well along in years.” '  — Luke 1:18

Zechariah, a righteous priest, doubted the angel. Zechariah spoke about the current facts, how he and his wife are now in their advanced age; barren.

Maybe Zechariah prayed for this exact day once in his life.
Maybe Zechariah gave up on that prayer a long time ago.
Sometimes, we have this prayer that we prayed for years and it never happened. 
And when we are in that place, in one way or another, we begin to doubt God and His love for us. Maybe starting to be skeptical or even disobey, for what God promised to you, felt impossible to you. We forget that His promises always come to pass, His love will never cease, and His word never fails.

> 'The angel said to him, “I am Gabriel. I stand in the presence of God, and I *have been sent to speak to you and to tell you this good news*. And now you will be ***silent and not able to speak until the day this happens***, because you did not believe my words, which will come true ***at their appointed time***.” '  — Luke 1:19-20

Sometimes, God will discipline us, or (biblically speaking) shut us up of our mouths, for the abundance of the heart, the mouth speaks. It was clear that Zechariah needed assurance. But God made Zechariah silent until the birth of John so Zechariah will never have to speak doubt about the promises of God.

We somehow shut off belief because of the current circumstances around us. And we might think that we're in a new season where a prayer you once prayed will no longer be useful or relevant.

God created laws of nature to govern the earth and make everything in order. But even the laws of nature bow down to the Word of God. If it's from the Father's will, he can bend the laws to make it happen.

God sees, and God provides. And as long as you're serving the Lord, He will give you anything to love you, and also glorify Him in the end.

Afterwards, there's the contrast between Zechariah and Mary—in the same chapter.

> '“How will this be,” Mary asked the angel, “since I am a virgin?” The angel answered, “***The Holy Spirit will come on you***, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. So the holy one to be born will be called the Son of God. Even Elizabeth your relative is going to have a child in her old age, and she who was said to be unable to conceive is in her sixth month. ***For no word from God will ever fail***.” '  — Luke 1:34-37

Notice the difference of the responses between Mary and Zechariah. Both prayers came to pass.

Zechariah asked in skeptical unbelief. Mary asked in wonder-filled faith.

> '“***I am the Lord’s servant***,” Mary answered. “***May your word to me be fulfilled***.” Then the angel left her. '  — Luke 1:38

Zechariah with priesthood credentials, was skeptical about God's promise.
Mary, with no credentials, asserted that she is a servant of the Lord—not to debate the Master, but to have His way in her.
Both have different backgrounds, both have been favored, but both have different responses.
Mary instantly knew that as she is bearing a child without a legal father. She would have to face persecutions, accusations of adultery. We do not know the weight she's been carrying, not just physically, but emotionally, psychologically, and spiritually.

Could you imagine bearing that as you go? But what did Mary say?

***"Let it be to me according to Your word"***. The proper response to every promise of God. You don't have to know everything. You just have His way to you. You don't have to offer him immediately—although He deserves your whole you. But he wants you to partner with him, and to trust Him. And because God's nature is faithful. His Word will never fail.

> 'for he has been ***mindful of the humble state of his servant***. From now on all generations will call me blessed, '  — Luke 1:48

To have someone be mindful of the humble state of His servant, is nothing short of caring. Mary is blessed, for she is carrying the Son of God, Jesus the Messiah.
