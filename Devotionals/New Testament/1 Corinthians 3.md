> *'I had to feed you with milk, not with solid food, because you weren’t ready for anything stronger. **And you still aren’t ready, for you are still controlled by your sinful nature**. You are jealous of one another and quarrel with each other. Doesn’t that prove you are controlled by your sinful nature? **Aren’t you living like people of the world**? '  — 1 Corinthians 3:2-3*  

Paul expresses his frustration by the Corinth's unspiritual dealings with each other and with God. He had to package the Gospel (feed you with milk and not solid food). He had to teach them the basics because the church believed that they're now equipped to learn something deeper.

Paul corrected them and highlighted a behavior of their hearts.

> *'When one of you says, “I am a follower of Paul,” and another says, “I follow Apollos,” **aren’t you acting just like people of the world**? '  — 1 Corinthians 3:4*

Their boasting about following a particular human leader caused division in the city when they should have been united with Christ.

You can be eternally saved but behaving like the people of the world.
You can end up following someone but misplacing Christ at the center.
You can want for something very deep but completely miss the heart of Jesus and His purpose for the world which is the basic foundation of major world faith.

> 'After all, who is Apollos? Who is Paul? We are only God’s servants ***through whom*** you believed the Good News. Each of us did the work the Lord gave us. ' — 1 Corinthians 3:5

Paul and Apollos are not the ones who delivered salvation for you. There are not the ones who you believed on in order to be saved.

Paul highlighted that they are only God's servants **through whom** you believed; not **on whom** you believed.

Each of us have different assignments God assigned to us—different callings, gifts, and anointing.
But this does not mean one had diminished assignment from the other.
Nor do they have the right to diminish each other's assignments.

> 'I planted the seed in your hearts, and Apollos watered it, but it was God who made it grow. It’s not important who does the planting, or who does the watering. What’s important is that God makes the seed grow. '  — 1 Corinthians 3:6-7  

After all, we have one common denominator of a purpose—to bring about the glory of the Lord and to bring Good News to people who are unworthy.

After all, we rely to the transformative power of God to grow the seed.
In the end, we still need to trust God. For no one can lay any foundation other than the one we already have—Jesus Christ.
