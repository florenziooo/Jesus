I am the true vine, and my Father is the gardener. He cuts of every branch in me that bears no fruit, while every branch that does bear fruit he prunes so that it will be even more fruitful. ([[John 15#^v1|John 15:1-2]] NIV)

I have a farm in my province. Ever since I was a kid, I always wondered why is “pagpamul-ong” (pruning a plant) was necessary, because it looked like hurting the tree. But sooner than I expected, the tree began to bear nangka, unlike the other trees that were never pruned and bore nothing.

My father explained to me: a plant’s branches draws energy too. And too much of it will spare no room for fruit. When a plant grows too dense, its inner leaves don’t get enough air or light. Without pruning, plants may grow wildly with many leaves —but no fruit. Yes, pruning causes pain, but those cuts lead to new shoots, blossoms, and life.

It’s the same with walking through life with Jesus. The Gardener sometimes prunes branches in life I thought were important to give way for those that TRULY MATTER. Truthfully, it is really messy and painful, but it’s never without purpose. Right now, I believe I’m in the season where I’m being pruned of my old habits, unhealthy attachments, and misplaced priorities. Yet I choose to take heart, have faith, and trust the process. Because I have a Gardener in heaven who lovingly prunes me—not to harm me, but to make way for growth.

When the storm comes—as it literally will, we must be ready to be pruned. Because when a plant is pruned, it is less resistive to the wind when pushed against it harder, preventing of being uprooted.
