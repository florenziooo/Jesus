### John the Baptist prepares the Way

> 'John announced: “Someone is coming soon who is greater than I am—so much greater that I’m not even worthy to stoop down like a slave and **untie the straps of his sandals**. '  — Mark 1:7 

It may seem like an overinflation of John the Baptist to say that, but to his awe and reverence of God, he acknowledges that he isn't worthy to be his follower, let alone a slave. The rabbis taught that a teacher might require just about anything of his disciples—except to make them untie of his sandals. John said he isn't even worthy to do this for Jesus.

John had a platform to prepare the way for Jesus. But this platform points not of John but of Jesus. I should never forget that the platform that we're stepping on shall point to Jesus. And how privileged am I to be able to be granted this opportunity!

The reason why Jesus wanted to be baptized by John not because he wanted to be cleansed of His sins—He is sinless—but so that He would be identified with sinners.
- He physically roamed among humanity.
- He existed so that Jews and Gentiles could see His face when others couldn't believed in God.
- He was able to see the reason why people sin.
- He was able to sympathize with the sinners.
- He was able to feel things a human could feel.
- He was able to be tempted.
- He was able to see the humanity in us.
- But He established a covenant with us—not just a public contract or announcement.
- That even when we fail, He is still faithful.
- That even when we fall short, He still loves.
- That even when we're stuck, He still offers a way out.
- And baptism is a public announcement to all that He would endure in His humanity until the end of His life to be in union with us—like a bridegroom vowing to his bride.
- And sadly, not all of us are willing.


> *'“The time promised by God has come at last!” he announced. “The Kingdom of God is near! **Repent of your sins and believe the Good News**!” '  — Mark 1:15*

He offered an opportunity to repent and change our way around. Repentance is not about feeling sorry about yourself or other people. Repentance is changing of mindset—changing of direction. Forgiveness is a gift given from the other person. But repentance comes from your decision.

If you sin against another person, when you felt sorry about it and promised never to do it again, it is up to the other person whether they forgive you or not.
It is not just promising to never do it again, it is actually redirecting your focus to what really matters. If you actually focus on Jesus and have your heart, thoughts, ideas, desires, and habits surrendered to Him, the Spirit of God will change you from the inside out.


> *'Amazement gripped the audience, and they began to discuss what had happened. “What sort of new teaching is this?” they asked excitedly. “**It has such authority! Even evil spirits obey his orders**!” '  — Mark 1:27*

We know about Jesus and His power to the point that we become overfamiliar to it. Even evil spirits recognized that He is God. Even evil spirits obey His orders. How soon are we to see God for who He is? How soon are we to obey to His orders?


> *'Jesus called out to them, “Come, follow me, and I will show you how **to fish for people**!” '  — Mark 1:17*  

He called us to be in partnership with the greatest movement on earth. He doesn't need us. He wants us. He wants us to be a part of the greatest commission. He wanted us to fulfill our true purpose in life—because He is the life.

> *'A man with leprosy came and knelt in front of Jesus, begging to be healed. “**If you are willing**, you can heal me and make me clean,” he said. '  — Mark 1:40*

Leprosy is the worst disease you can get in this time. It is pretty much synonymous with a dead person. With skin burns and inflammations in the whole body—what you used to feel compassion for the sick may not apply here. You repulse when you're around a leper.

But Jesus did not hold back and felt compassion towards the leper.

This leper all throughout his life thought no one can heal him of his illness. No one can cure. No one can accept him for who he is. There's no cure to leprosy at that time.

But when he saw Jesus, he began to see hope again. He understand that this Man can make his illness bow down to His word. The leper understood the Lord's sovereignty. But he also understands that it is only up to the Lord's choice to heal him—for he felt unworthy to be even near even anyone at all.

That's why he prefaced his petition by saying "If you are willing".

He healed him instantly.

> *'“**Don’t tell anyone about this**. Instead, go to the priest and let him examine you. Take along the offering required in the law of Moses for those who have been healed of leprosy. This will be a public testimony that you have been cleansed.” **But the man went and spread the word**, proclaiming to everyone what had happened. As a result, large crowds soon surrounded Jesus, and he couldn’t publicly enter a town anywhere. He had to stay out in the secluded places, but people from everywhere kept coming to him.'  — Mark 1:44-45*

The leper went anyway and disobeyed the Lord immediately right after He healed him. May be out of his amazement or shock, he began spreading the word prematurely in the wrong time.

That's why it is important to listen closely to the His word and to be obedient to His orders. Sometimes, we don't know what we're doing. We often do what we do because it feels right. You might feel the urgency to let other people know. But there are orders of the Lord that we must obey even if it is not in our understanding yet.

But I must ask, Lord, if you knew what would happen next, why were you willing?
