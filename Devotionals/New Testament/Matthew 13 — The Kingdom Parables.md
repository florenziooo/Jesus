## Parable of the Farmer Scattering Seed

There are seeds that fell on different places.

“Some seeds fell on the footpath, and the BIRDS came and ate them.”

The birds represent the enemies.

“Some seeds fell on shallow soil with underlying rock. The seeds sprouted quickly because the soil was shallow. But the plants soon wilted under the hot sun, and since they didn’t have deep roots, they withered.”

There are people who joyfully received God’s message. But because they aren’t deep rooted enough to the heavenly foundation, they quickly fell away when trials hit them.

“Other seeds fell among thorns that grew up and choked up the tender plants.”

Thorns represent the worldly distractions that hindered the people of their growth and maturity. This might mean distractions that invoke pride, defensiveness, and disobedience.

“Still, other seeds fell on fertile soil, and they produced a crop that was thirty, sixty, and even a hundred times as much as had been planted!”

There are people who received God’s word AND truly understood it. This, in turn, produces fruit in multitudes people couldn’t imagine producing just by themselves.

We already knew that Jesus is the sower of seeds, but notice that those seeds are under the same sun. The sun represents the truth. It is constant; it never changes. The problem is not the sun, but the conditions where the seeds are situated [the conditions of our hearts]

The same sun that softens the wax hardens the clay.

Jesus never threated people to come near Him. He simply illustrates the kingdom truth in a worldly sense. Then it puts the responsibility fairly to the individual. It reveals truth to whoever who desires truth, and it conceals truth from whoever does not desire the truth.

That is why Jesus spoke to them in parables. For truth is a sword that divides people in half. The truth is revealed to the humble, but is concealed to the proud. Just like wax, a humble heart melts, and just like clay, a proud heart resists.

## The Parable of the Wheat and Weeds

The field = the world
The wheat = children of the Kingdom
The tares = children of the evil one
The harvest = the end of the age
The reapers = angels
The farmer = God
The workers = leaders

“Should we pull out the weeds?”, the farmer’s workers asked.

Even the farmer’s workers [leaders] seek God’s wisdom ([[Matthew 6#^v33|Matthew 6:33]]). For they already know that the human judgment is limited; God is able to see the heart perfectly.

“‘No,’ he replied, ‘you’ll uproot the wheat if you do. Let both grow together until the harvest. Then I will tell the harvesters to sort out the weeds, tie them into bundles, and burn them, and to put the wheat in the barn.’”

This means when discipling a person, premature judgment crushes a young believer. The final judgment belongs to God alone.

Be patient and don’t jump into conclusions.

What looks like a weed today may still be wheat. Tearing it too early can destroy what God is growing.

## The Parables of the Mustard Seed and the Yeast

“The kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed, which a man took and planted in his field. Though it is the smallest of all seeds, yet when it grows, it is the largest of garden plants and becomes a tree, so that the birds come and perch in its branches.”

“The kingdom of heaven is like yeast that a woman took and mixed into about sixty pounds flour until it worked all through the dough.”

These two parables signify how important it is to spread God’s message to a person—even an atheist. For any fruitful Christian comes from a seed as small as a mustard planted in him. And that man can benefit and impact multitudes. For any leaven dough formed comes from a tiny amount of yeast, enough to permeate the whole flour.

## Jesus Rejected at Nazareth

“Where did this man get this wisdom and these miraculous powers?”

“Isn’t this the carpenter’s son? Isn’t his mother’s name Mary, and aren’t his brothers James, Joseph, Simon and Judas?

A lot were surprised at Jesus of his fruit simply because his prejudiced hometown were already familiar of his origins—an ordinary young boy; nothing good comes out of Nazareth”.

Because of Jesus’ success and glory, a lot were offended. This sparked a CRAB MENTALITY just because of pride and prejudice that blinded them from seeing the truth.

“He did not do many miracles there because of their unbelief.”

Not because Jesus was powerless, but because their unbelief limited what they could potentially receive.

We get stuck to familiar places where we get easily blinded of change. Oftentimes it happens to the people close to us—maybe our family or close friends. They struggle to accept the change in you because they thought they knew better and knew of your nature. But because God is so powerful He can change and free you from the sinful nature the world labels you to be in.

Just as Jesus did, we must continue of our mission in accordance to our faith. We don’t need to forcefully prove them that we have changed. Sometimes it’s better to walk away and pray for them instead.
