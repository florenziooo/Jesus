> [!quote] [[John 8#^v12|John 8:12]]
> “I am the light of the world. Whoever follows Me will never walk in darkness, but will have the light of life.”

Let’s say that you were tasked to draw a tennis ball. First, you will draw a circle. But a tennis ball isn’t just a circle. It’s a sphere. So, how do you draw a sphere?

You begin with a circle, then you add shadows. Next, you add tiny occlusions from the hairs of the ball; maybe soft silhouettes around the edges.

As the artist, when you draw those shadows, you’re actually suggesting that there’s a light coming from somewhere.

In art, you don’t draw light itself, rather, you draw it’s effect.

You can never appreciate light without first seeing the darkness.
You can never appreciate beauty without seeing what’s broken.
You can never appreciate joy without known sorrow.
You can never appreciate happiness without having felt pain.

If the ball had no shadows. It would just be a flat circle. Lifeless. Empty.

That’s exactly why God gave us free will. He doesn’t want us to love Him forcefully like robots. It would be like a drawing with no depth. Just flat.

> [!quote] [[Matthew 5#^v16|Matthew 5:16]]
> “Let your light shine before others, that they may see your good deeds and glorify your Father in heaven.”

When the shading on the tennis ball is finally complete, the sphere comes alive—something other people can clearly see.

When God works through your shadows…
People see transformation.
People see compassion.
People see hope in you.
And what they’re really seeing is a reflection of Him—the Light!

> [!quote] [[Psalm 23#^v4|Psalm 23:4]]
> “Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me…”

Your shadows don’t define you.
Your shadows reveal the direction of the Light.
